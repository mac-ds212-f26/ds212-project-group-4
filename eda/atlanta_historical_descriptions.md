
## Grade A 

- **A1** — Tuxedo Park, West Paces Ferry Rd. and Peachtree Heights Park (all outside of city)
  a-southern portion; b-northern portion. This is community's most exclusive resiential area, in which live some of the city's most wealthy families. Large estates in area. Peachtree Heights Park was developed about 20 years ago and hence contains older properties than in the Tuxedo Park section which is to a certain extent drawing from Peachtree Heights Park. Property if acquired in this area should be held for fair value.

- **A2** — Southwestern portion of Peachtree Heights Park, Westover, and Haynes Manor (all outside of city)

  (a) - Peachtree Battle Avenue, Dellwood Drive, and a portion of Manor Ridge Drive were improved with houses prior to depression, but majority of the houses in the area have been erected since the depression. Restrictions in southern portion of area, along Woodward Way and Dellwood Drive, are lower than in remainder of area. Property if acquired in this area should be held for fair value.

- **A3** — Newer portion of Garden Hills (outside of city)
  Property if acquired in this area should be held for fair value.

- **A4** — Peachtree Park (outside of city)
  (a) This is new subdivision containing about 120 lots (40 of which has been sold) on which have been erected about 20 houses for sale. About 8 of the 20 are under construction or are completed but are held vacant for sale. Considerable speculative building activity in this area. Property if acquired in this area should be held for fair value.

- **A5** — Ridgedale Park (outside of city)
  This subdivision has been largely developed since depression and contains about 40 houses. Property, if acquired in this area, should be held for fair value.

- **A6** — Known as H. L. DeFoor Property
  This area was developed prior to the depression as a high-class residential section, but most of the houses in the area have been erected since the depression. Property, if acquired in this area, should be held for fair value.

- **A7** — Brookhaven Estates and Country Club Estates (outside city - majority in Fulton County, with small postion in DeKalb County)

  This area was developed prior to the depression as a high-class residential section, but most of the houses in the area have been erected since the depression. Property, if acquired in this area, should be held for fair value.

- **A8** — Lenox Park, Johnson Estates, and Hyland Park (Lenox Park is in Fulton County, while Johnson Estates and Hyland Park are in DeKalb County; most of Hyland Park is outside city limits)
  Practically all of the houses in this area have been erected since the depression. Property, if acquired in this area, should be held for fair value.

- **A9** — Glennwood Estates (in DeKalb County and City of Decatur)
  This is new extension of subdivision which was developed about 10 years ago (B-19), hence contains only new construction. Practically all of the houses have been erected during past 2 years. Property, if acquired in this area, should be held for fair value.


## Grade B 

- **B1** — Paces Ferry Road Development Company's property (outside city)
  This area contains mixture of one and two-story residences (majority built about 10 yrs ago), while present new construction in area is of speculative two-story type. Little possibility that this area will witness much higher values; hence, property if acquired in this area, should be sold. Southern portion of this area is rear portion of, estates fronting on Peachtree Road.

- **B2** — Older portion of Peachtree Heights Park (outside of city)
  There are two large apartment houses in this area on Peachtree Road, with apartments renting for $50-$75 per mo. Property, if acquired in this area, should be sold rather than held.

- **B3** — Northern portion of Peachtree Terrace and all of Peachtree Heights (outside city)
  This area contains a mixture of several types of property; some is new and in excellent repair conditions, while some is old and in only fair repair condition. most of new activity has been confined to easter portion of the area. Property if acquired in western portion of area, should be sold; if in easter portion, should be held for fair value.

- **B4** — Older portion of Garden Hills (outside city)
  Property, if acquired in this area, should be sold rather than held.

- **B5** — Peachtree Highlands and Piedmont Road section (outside city)

- **B6** — Peachtree Rd.-Roxboro Rd. section (outside city)
  This area contains a mixture of expensive property along Peachtree Road and considerable cheaper property between Lennox and Roxboro Roads (south of Peachtree Road). Property, if acquired in this section, should be held for fair value.

- **B7** — Wieuca Road portion and Northridge Park (outside city)

- **B8** — Spring Lake Park and Collier Road section (outside city)

- **B9** — A.J. Collier Estate Subdivision and Brookwood (northern portion outside city)

- **B11** — Ansley Park Annex and northern portion of Ansley Park

- **B12** — Collier Woods and Ansley Park

This is the oldest still desirable residential area in city and although many og the properties are quite old, there is a good demand for property in the area- principally from rental standpoint. Future outlook for property in this area is slowly downward; hence, if property is acquired in this area, it should be sold rather than held.

- **B13** — Morningside, Rock Springs Rd setion and better portions of North Boulevard Park, North Highlands and Virginia Highlands

- **B14** — Druid Hills (Majority outside city - all of area in DeKalb County)

Some of the community's finest homes and most substantial citizens reside in large expensive homes on both sides of Ponce de Leon Avenue; however, the site of these properties makes sale of same difficult.

- **B15** — B15

- **B17** — Clairmont Estates and Ponce de Leon Heights (All in DeKalb County and majority in City of Decatur)

N-W portion of area, excluding Clairmont Av frontage, is Clairmont Estates subdivision, recently opened, and contains 5 or 6 new houses, half of which have been built for sale some 90 days and have not yet been sold. The new Atlanta to Lawrenceville Highway, cutting off Decatur, is now being constructed and skirts the northwest boundary of the area.

## Grade C 

- **C1** — Buckhead Section (outside city)
  Area contains several large apartment houses as well as large churches and business property intermingled along Peachtree Road frontage. The apartments have good rental demand since occupants escape city taxation. The wide range in family incomes is due to presence of a few well-to-do families who still occupy homes in the area, plus the tenants in the apartments who earn good salaries, ranging downward to lower income groups along Peachtree Avenue, Woodland Street and Lookout Place. From home-ownership standpoint, property, if acquired in this area, should be sold; however, in cases where zoning and restrictions permit, it might be better to hold for higher values occasioned by use of property for business purposes.

- **C2** — Brookhaven and Brookhaven Heights (outside city and in DeKalb County)
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold as quicly as possible.

- **C3** — Peachtree Road section, Peachtree Hills, and southern portion of Peachtree Heights (outside city)
  Property if acquired in this area, should be sold rather than held. Residents in higher income bracket live in apartments along Peachtree Road. One large apartment house was erected about a year ago. A limited dividend housing project (known as Peachtree Hills Apartment) is now under construction immediately east of this area at an approximate cost of $800,000, financed by F H A Title II loan for $640,000, made by New York Life Insurance Company. When completed, the project will house 174 families.

- **C4** — Peachtree Road section between Brookwood and Seaboard RR (majority outside city)
  This is principally an apartment house section, apartments in this area having a good rental demand, since most of the apartments are outside the city and the occupants escape city taxation. Wide variation of family income is due to the fact that some of the community's well-to-do citizens still live in original homes in area. Property if acquired in this area should be sold rather than held.

- **C5** — C5
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold rather than held.

- **C6** — Section north of Fourteenth Street between Piedmont Park and Spring Street to Brookwood Station
  Property if acquired in this area, should be sold rather than held. Most of city's largest apartment houses are in this area which is desirable rental area because of transportation facilities and proximity to center of city.

- **C7** — Section between Sixth and Sixteenth Streets and State and Spring Sts
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold rather than held.

- **C8** — Section between Ponce de Leon Ave and Fourteenth St. and Spring Street and Southern RR Belt Line
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold rather than held. (a) Wide spread of income due to fact that some of city's older business men still reside in original homes in the area. (b) Present sales prices based principally on purchase of homes for boarding houses on investment basis-not for home ownership. (c) Comparatively high rental prices pertain principally to northeast section because of proximity to schools.

- **C9** — Section between Ponce de Leon and Forrest Aves and Parkway Drive and Sou. R.R. Belt Line
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold as quickly as possible. (a) Due to Sears-Roebuck rental effect in eastern portion of area.

- **C10** — North Boulevard, between Amsterdam Avenue and Montgomery Ferry Road
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold rather than held.

- **C11** — Ponce de Leon Avenue to Edgewood Ave. between Moreland Ave. and Southern Railroad belt line (includes Linwood, Copenhill, and Inman Park)
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold rather than held

- **C12** — Highland Avenue section from Ponce de Leon Avenue to University Drive

- **C13** — Hancock subdivision, Atkins Park, western edge of Druid Hills, and University Park (portion of this area is in Fulton County and portion in DeKalb; portion also outside of city)

- **C16** — Emory University section and northeast portion of Druid Hills (outside city - in DeKalb County)
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold rather than held.

- **C17** — Southern portion of Emory Highlands and Chelsea Heights (both are outside of city and are in DeKalb County)
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold rather than held. Southern portion of area (Chelsea Heights) is resurrected subdivision. Now being developed and contains about 5 or 6 new residences built within last two years, two of which still remain unsold. Apparently there is little sales demand for property in this rough, heavily wooded subdivision, through which an open creek runs.

- **C18** — Older portion of Clairmont Park, Clairmont Road frontage, and Sycamore Heights (in DeKalb County, city of Decatur)
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold. (a) Very few sales in this type of property in last 10 years. Extreme eastern portion contains cheaper type property and occupants than elsewhere in area. Due to the desire of Decatur's older families to live on Sycamore Street, good demand exists for property on same, regardless of age. This same condition also exists to a lesser degree on Clairmont Avenue and South Candler Street in C-20.

- **C19** — Avondale Estates (in DeKalb County - City of Avondale)
  Property if acquired in this area should be sold rather than held. This subdivision was developed on an extensive scale prior to the depression, but has witness no new residential construction for the past 9 years. During the depression the owner of the subdivision became financilly involved and at public sale lots sold at 10 to 20% of original sales price and in many instances sold at less than cost of paving assessments against the lots. However, the $7.500 building restrictions are still in force, which has prevented the construction of cheaper type homes. While this area has witnessed drastic shrinkage of values and heavy foreclosures, it is a desirable residential area from many standpoints and it is believed that the subdivision's desirability and values will stabilize at about present levels.

- **C20** — Oakhurst and older portion of Decatur (in DeKalb County and city of Decatur)
